Programs Direction for PG-FACES

A program is a goal cut into steps. An agent does each step in its own worktree; a verified check, a fresh reviewer and your press (or the delegate) take it to merged. What waits on you is always on top. Design: docs/internal/betterment/programs/PROGRAMS-DESIGN-2026-10-28.md §9. Every part below is a kit piece at real size; the list is in CANVAS-NOTES-2026-10-28.md.

Recommendation: C, the ledger. Tuesday, 18:00, twenty steps: you want the whole program on one screen, the red and the ones that wait on you found in one look, and the words only where you look. C gives each step one 32 px row, so twenty steps take 640 px and fit a 1440 × 900 window with the well and the status bar. The six dots say where a step is; the one word beside them says its state (Merge?, Check failed, Merged #1591); the lit dot breathes. The full track with its words is on the step you select (the Gate fold), not repeated twenty times.

A draws the six words on every row: it reads well for six steps and doubles the height (twenty steps need 1 160 px). B is the most friendly picture and the slowest to scan: three columns of cards make your eye zig-zag, the order (step 1 to 20) breaks at every line, and a card per step is the card-in-card the grammar forbids inside a window.

What this canvas could not establish

  • The breath at the board's scale is judged live: a 10 px dot with a 5 px ring in a dense row may read as too busy with three steps lit at once. If it does, only the step in your selection breathes and the others stay lit and still.
  • The tokens and minutes column waits on the harness: the design shows them only when the harness reports them, so this canvas draws minutes only.
  • The desk floor is drawn in CSS (not a recorded shot), so the floor icons are absent; the windows, the menu bar and the dock use the kit's values.
